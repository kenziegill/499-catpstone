ANSWER

The system indicates these aren't direct contradictions, likely because they refer to different device types (mobile phones vs tablets). Here's what the research shows about mobile checkout:

## CTA Placement
- Mobile users prefer the primary CTA above the fold - checkout completion was faster when the Place Order button was visible without scrolling [f-b9016884]
- However, tablet users behaved differently, scrolling past above-the-fold CTAs and only engaging when the button was placed below the fold near the order total [f-9ec44e6f]

## Guest Checkout Visibility
- Guest checkout is difficult to find for first-time users, with most participants attempting to back out before locating it, especially on smaller devices where it appears below the fold [f-b053f3af]
- Tablet users face similar issues with guest checkout placement in the lower-right corner [f-94a7bda3]
- The horizontal layout that works on phones breaks down at tablet width, making guest checkout harder to find [f-10c15b23]

## Touch Target Sizing
- Quantity adjustment buttons and dropdowns use 24px instead of the 44px touch minimum, causing mis-taps and accidental item removals [f-e1c1aeb9]

## Pricing Transparency
- Late disclosure of shipping costs (on payment screen) causes negative reactions and potential abandonment [f-26d7ccf1]

The research shows mobile checkout has significant usability issues around guest checkout discoverability, touch target sizing, and CTA placement varies by device size.
